\begin{abstract}
Standard Transformer self-attention normalizes inner products via the strictly positive Softmax operator on the open probability simplex $\Delta^{L-1}$. Without a mechanism for selective subspace sign inversion in the pre-Softmax logits, historical tokens retain unattenuated inner-product contributions even when superseded or contradicted by subsequent discourse, causing premise distraction in long sequences. We present \textbf{Subspace Anti-Phase RoPE (SG-M\"obius-RoPE)}, an attention formulation extending Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) via causal orthogonal subspace phase modulation. Drawing an analogy to acoustic Active Noise Cancellation (ANC), the model introduces an out-of-phase anti-carrier on low-frequency evolution channels while freezing high-frequency syntactic anchors ($\mathbb{R}^d = \mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$). Causal semantic transitions induce a continuous phase velocity $\omega_{h,m}(t) \in [-\omega_{\max}, \omega_{\max}]$ whose cumulative unconstrained phase $\bar{\Phi}(t) = \sum_{\tau=1}^t \omega(\tau)$ is shaped by a training-free phase-locking quantizer $\Phi(t) = \bar{\Phi}(t) - \eta\sin(2\bar{\Phi}(t))$. Over narrative intervals undergoing logical inversion, cumulative phase shift reaches $\pi$, producing exact destructive sign inversion ($\mathbf{R}(\theta - \pi) = -\mathbf{R}(\theta)$) in pre-Softmax logits without altering KV-cache invariance. Successive restorative turns accumulate a $2\pi$ cyclic phase rotation ($\cos 2\pi = +1$), formalizing double negation restoration. We prove the Conditional Pairwise Target-to-Decoy Odds Ratio Attenuation Theorem, establishing that under coherent evolution-subspace alignment ($A_{ij}^{\text{mob}} > 0$), the single-head pairwise odds ratio scales by $\exp(2A_{ij}^{\text{mob}})$ with exact cancellation of the head partition function. Invariants are formally certified in Lean~4 (0 axioms, 0 \texttt{sorry}) and Z3 SMT solvers across 2,000 extreme-length sequences ($L \le 32,768$). Evaluated on Qwen3.5-9B (dual Nvidia Tesla T4 GPUs, FP16), SG-M\"obius requires zero architecture retraining, maintains near-identical $O(1)$ KV-cache decoding latency via epilogue angle fusion ($1.8\text{--}2.1\,\mu\text{s}$ per-layer operator overhead), achieves strictly monotonic decoy attention suppression ($1.13\times\text{--}1.90\times$) across multi-scale contexts (expanding multi-head Attention Contrast Ratio by up to $+465.1\%$ from $3.62 \to 20.44$, with $1.34\times$ suppression and $+88.5\%$ ACR gain at $L=18,030$ tokens, and reaching up to $16.4\times$ peak odds amplification on individual retrieval heads on Layer~7, Head~4), and strictly preserves neutral text perplexity ($7.48$, $0.0\%$ degradation).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Autoregressive language models employ scaled dot-product attention \cite{vaswani2017attention} with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) \cite{su2024roformer} to encode relative token distances:
\begin{equation}
A_{i,j} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \langle \mathbf{R}_{\Theta, i} q_i, \, \mathbf{R}_{\Theta, j} k_j \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} q_i^\top \mathbf{R}_{\Theta, j-i} k_j
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R}_{\Theta, t}$ is a block-diagonal orthogonal rotation matrix parameterized by base frequencies $\Theta = \{\theta_m\}_{m=1}^{d/2}$.

Because the Softmax operator maps real logits to strictly positive values on $\Delta^{L-1}$, every historical token receives positive probability mass. Consequently, self-attention cannot perform destructive interference. In complex discourse, premises are frequently invalidated, retracted, or superseded by subsequent evidence (e.g., \emph{``System Alpha is operational; however, diagnostics indicate failure, revoking Alpha in favor of Beta''}). In standard attention, the invalidated premise continues to receive attention mass, requiring downstream feed-forward layers to compensate and contributing to premise hallucination \cite{liu2024lost, hsieh2024ruler}.

To address this structural constraint without retraining pretrained foundation weights or degrading inference latency, we introduce \textbf{Subspace Anti-Phase RoPE (SG-M\"obius-RoPE)}, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:intro_teaser}. Drawing inspiration from Active Noise Cancellation (ANC), we decompose the embedding space into an orthogonal direct sum $\mathbb{R}^d = \mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$. Semantic contrastive transitions induce a continuous phase velocity $\omega_{h,m}(t) \in [-\omega_{\max}, \omega_{\max}]$ with viscous smoothing and a phase-locking potential projection $-\eta\sin(2\bar{\Phi})$, whose cumulative phase flow $\Phi(t)$ smoothly modulates 2D rotary angles on evolution channels (Figure~\ref{fig:curvature}). Over narrative intervals undergoing logical inversion, cumulative phase shift produces destructive interference ($\Delta \Phi = \pi$, $\cos\pi = -1$) via planar rotation sign reversal $\mathbf{R}(\theta - \pi) = -\mathbf{R}(\theta)$. Because $\cos(\pi) = -1$, the modulated inner product becomes negative, attenuating the Softmax probability allocated to the invalidated premise at arbitrary reversal coordinates (Figure~\ref{fig:arbitrary_heatmap}).

\paragraph{Key Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Subspace Anti-Phase Formulation}: We formalize attention modulation via orthogonal subspace decomposition $\mathbb{R}^d = \mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$ and planar anti-phase rotation $\mathbf{R}(\theta - \pi) = -\mathbf{R}(\theta)$. Continuous phase flow $\Phi(t)$ modulates rotary angles $\tilde{\theta}(t) = t\theta + \Phi(t)$ on evolution channels while high-frequency syntactic anchor channels remain frozen ($\alpha$ ratio). We prove that continuous rotary dual modulation preserves relative translation invariance and establishes an explicit Lipschitz continuity bound on operator differences.
    \item \textbf{Conditional Pairwise Odds Ratio Attenuation Theorem}: We prove that under an anti-phase half-turn ($\Delta \Phi = \pi$) and coherent subspace alignment ($A_{ij}^{\text{mob}} > 0$), the single-head pairwise target-to-decoy attention odds ratio scales by $\exp(2 A_{ij}^{\text{mob}})$, strictly cancelling the background partition function within the head.
    \item \textbf{Formal Machine Verification}: We verify program equivalence between the two-stage associative scan and autoregressive recurrence, prefix-sum additivity, and phase shift invariance in Lean~4 (0 unproven axioms or \texttt{sorry}) and Z3 SMT solvers across 2,000 fuzzing sequences ($L \le 32,768$) with numerical drift below $1.14 \times 10^{-13}$.
    \item \textbf{KV-Cache Compatibility}: Modulated keys $\tilde{k}_j = \mathbf{R}(j\Theta + \Phi(j)) k_j$ depend strictly on past tokens $\tau \le j$, requiring no retroactive cache modification upon subsequent turns and preserving exact $O(1)$ decoding throughput.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation}: Evaluated natively on Qwen3.5-9B (dual Nvidia Tesla T4 GPUs, FP16), SG-M\"obius achieves strictly monotonic decoy attention suppression ($1.13\times\text{--}1.90\times$), up to $+465.1\%$ multi-head Attention Contrast Ratio gain ($3.62 \to 20.44$ at $L=1,116$, and $+88.5\%$ gain at $L=18,030$ tokens, reaching up to $16.4\times$ on single-head peak contrastive recall on Layer~7, Head~4), gracefully scales attention entropy with context volume, and strictly maintains neutral text perplexity ($7.48$, $0.0\%$ degradation).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Rotary Position Embeddings and Context Extension.}
Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) \cite{su2024roformer} encodes relative token positions through 2D block-diagonal rotation matrices $\mathbf{R}_{\Theta, m}$, ensuring that query-key inner products depend purely on relative coordinate displacements $j - i$. Subsequent extensions such as ALiBi \cite{press2021train}, Position Interpolation \cite{chen2023extending}, and YaRN \cite{peng2023yarn} extend effective context windows by modifying frequency bases or applying linear distance penalties. However, all existing RoPE variants operate with fixed positional frequencies where relative inner products cannot perform selective subspace sign inversion in pre-Softmax logits.

\paragraph{Phase Modulation and Subspace Geometry in Attention.}
Geometric deep learning \cite{bronstein2021geometric} provides a unified framework for incorporating physical and geometric symmetries into neural networks. In signal processing, Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) attenuates unwanted interference by generating an anti-phase replica of the carrier signal. While conventional RoPE extensions \cite{press2021train, chen2023extending, peng2023yarn} modify frequency bases or distance decays, they lack mechanisms for destructive sign inversion. In this work, we introduce causal orthogonal phase modulation into 2D rotary embedding subspaces, enabling selective destructive cancellation in pre-Softmax attention logits across semantic transitions.

\paragraph{Context Interference and Premise Invalidation.}
Recent empirical studies have identified context distraction and the inability to forget invalidated premises as critical bottlenecks in long-context language models \cite{liu2024lost, hsieh2024ruler, kamradt2023needle}. When an adversarial or conflicting premise is presented, models frequently suffer from premise hallucination because strictly positive Softmax attention cannot actively cancel historical tokens. Previous attempts to address this issue rely on prompt-based instruction tuning or auxiliary suppression losses, which do not address the architectural lack of pre-Softmax destructive subspace cancellation mechanisms.

\paragraph{KV-Cache Compression vs. Phase-Modulated Retention.}
To mitigate memory and latency bottlenecks in extended contexts, recent methods explore token eviction or KV-cache compression \cite{dao2022flashattention, xiao2023streamingllm, zhang2023h2o}. While effective for throughput, eviction policies permanently discard historical keys from physical GPU memory. Consequently, if a subsequent turn re-validates an earlier premise ($\neg\neg P \iff P$), evicted representations cannot be recovered. In contrast, SG-M\"obius preserves the intact KV-cache in GPU memory and achieves selective premise cancellation purely through anti-phase modulation, naturally supporting topological recovery under $2\pi$ cyclic phase restoration.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Causal Phase Flow, Active Noise Cancellation Analogy, and Subspace Decomposition}
Let the input sequence of length $L \in \mathbb{N}$ be defined over a 1D discrete causal index set:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M} = \left( \{0, 1, \dots, L-1\}, \, \le \right)
\end{equation}
where $\le$ denotes the standard causal ordering ($j \le i$ means key token $j$ precedes or equals query token $i$). In standard RoPE, the $d$-dimensional embedding space is decomposed into an orthogonal direct sum of $d/2$ two-dimensional planes:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{R}^d = \bigoplus_{m=1}^{d/2} \mathbb{R}^2_{(m)}
\end{equation}
Each plane $\mathbb{R}^2_{(m)} \cong \mathbb{C}$ is acted upon by the continuous planar rotation group $\mathrm{SO}(2) \cong \mathrm{U}(1)$ parameterized by relative positional shifts $\theta_m (j - i)$.

\paragraph{Planar Rotation Inversion and Active Noise Cancellation Analogy.}
Standard RoPE applies 2D planar rotation matrices $\mathbf{R}(\theta) \in \mathrm{SO}(2)$ to query and key components. Crucially, a rotation by $\pi$ achieves exact sign inversion in the 2D plane:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{R}(\pi) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\pi & -\sin\pi \\ \sin\pi & \cos\pi \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -I_2
\end{equation}
Notice that $\det(-I_2) = (-1)^2 = +1$, so $-I_2$ is an orientation-preserving planar rotation. By superimposing a cumulative phase shift $\Delta\Phi = \pi$ onto the positional carrier $(j - i)\theta_m$, we obtain:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{R}\big((j - i)\theta_m - \pi\big) = -\mathbf{R}\big((j - i)\theta_m\big)
\end{equation}
This mechanism functions in direct analogy to acoustic \emph{Active Noise Cancellation (ANC)}: by generating an out-of-phase anti-carrier ($\pi$ phase offset) on the relevant semantic frequency channels, the inner product undergoes destructive sign reversal. In the pre-Softmax logit space, this converts a large positive dot product into a negative logit, causing the Softmax probability mass to drop exponentially. Furthermore, a subsequent negation-of-negation turn introduces an additional $\pi$ shift ($\Delta\Phi = 2\pi$), where $\mathbf{R}\big((j - i)\theta_m - 2\pi\big) = +\mathbf{R}\big((j - i)\theta_m\big)$, seamlessly restoring nominal attention resonance without retroactive modifications to the KV-cache.

\paragraph{Subspace Decomposition and High-Frequency Freezing.}
To prevent semantic phase accumulation from destabilizing local syntactic dependencies and sentence structure, we introduce an orthogonal subspace decomposition:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{R}^d = \mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal{I} = \{1, \dots, d/2\}$ denote the set of 2D subspace indices. In RoPE, base frequencies follow $\theta_m = \theta_{\text{base}}^{-2(m-1)/d}$, where lower indices correspond to high rotary frequencies capturing short-range syntactic features and punctuation. We allocate an anchor ratio $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ (with the natural convention $\mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}} = \emptyset$ when $\alpha = 0$) and partition the indices into disjoint sets $\mathcal{I} = \mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}} \cup \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}} \triangleq \left\{1, \dots, \left\lfloor \alpha \frac{d}{2} \right\rfloor \right\}, \qquad \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}} \triangleq \left\{\left\lfloor \alpha \frac{d}{2} \right\rfloor + 1, \dots, \frac{d}{2} \right\}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} = \bigoplus_{m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}}} \mathbb{R}^2_{(m)}$ and $\mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}} = \bigoplus_{m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}} \mathbb{R}^2_{(m)}$.
For all channels $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}}$, we enforce $\omega_{h,m}(t) \equiv 0 \implies \Phi_{h,m}(t) \equiv 0$, guaranteeing strict syntactic stability. Semantic phase flow is accumulated exclusively on $\mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$.

\begin{definition}[Continuous Phase Velocity and Phase-Locking Quantizer]
For attention head $h$ and evolution subspace $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$, the raw causal phase velocity $\omega_{h,m}(t) \in [-\omega_{\max}, \omega_{\max}]$ is governed by 1D viscous smoothing:
\begin{equation}
\omega_{h,m}(t) = (1 - \nu)\omega_{h,m}(t-1) + \nu g_{h,m}(h_t), \quad \bar{\Phi}_{h,m}(t) \triangleq \sum_{\tau=1}^t \omega_{h,m}(\tau)
\end{equation}
where $\nu \in (0, 1]$ is the viscosity coefficient and $g_{h,m}(h_t) = \omega_{\max}\tanh(W_g h_t + b_g)$ extracts local semantic transition density using a fixed, training-free contrastive anchor projection $W_g$ derived from the pretrained unembedding subspace of discourse negation markers. To suppress small local phase jitter near multiples of $\pi$ without breaking parallel prefix-sum associativity of $\bar{\Phi}_{h,m}(t)$ during batch prefill, the effective rotary phase $\Phi_{h,m}(t)$ applies a pointwise memoryless staircase projection derived from the periodic potential $V(\bar{\Phi}) = -\frac{\eta}{2}\cos(2\bar{\Phi})$ ($\eta \in [0, \tfrac{1}{2}]$):
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{h,m}(t) \triangleq \bar{\Phi}_{h,m}(t) - \eta\sin\!\big(2\bar{\Phi}_{h,m}(t)\big)
\end{equation}
In our default head-coherent configuration, all evolution channels $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$ within head $h$ share a common head-level phase velocity trajectory $\omega_h(t)$ and phase $\Phi_h(t)$. The relative causal phase shift between key $j$ and query $i$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Phi_{j\to i}^{(m)} \triangleq \Phi_m(i) - \Phi_m(j)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Continuous Rotary Dual Modulation}
In SG-M\"obius-RoPE, the smooth phase flow $\Phi(t)$ directly modulates the 2D rotary angle $\tilde{\theta}_m(t) \triangleq t\theta_m + \Phi_m(t)$ in each subspace.

\begin{definition}[Continuous Rotary Dual Modulation]
For each 2D subspace $m \in \{1, \dots, d/2\}$ with base frequency $\theta_m$, the modulated query and key representations at positions $i$ and $j$ are defined as:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{q}_i^{(m)} \triangleq \mathbf{R}(i\theta_m + \Phi_m(i)) q_i^{(m)}, \qquad \tilde{k}_j^{(m)} \triangleq \mathbf{R}(j\theta_m + \Phi_m(j)) k_j^{(m)}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R}(\theta) \in \mathrm{SO}(2)$ denotes the planar orthogonal rotation matrix. Let the baseline RoPE-rotated inner products on $\mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}}$ and $\mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$ be denoted by:
\begin{align}
\langle q_i, \, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}} &\triangleq \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}}} (q_i^{(m)})^\top \mathbf{R}\big((j - i)\theta_m\big) k_j^{(m)}, \\
\langle q_i, \, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}} &\triangleq \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}} (q_i^{(m)})^\top \mathbf{R}\big((j - i)\theta_m\big) k_j^{(m)}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Preservation of Relative Position and Phase Shift Invariance under Subspace Decomposition]
\label{thm:trans_inv}
Under continuous rotary dual modulation with subspace decomposition $\mathbb{R}^d = \mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$, the inner product between query $\tilde{q}_i$ and key $\tilde{k}_j$ depends purely on relative positional displacement $j - i$ and relative phase shift $\Delta \Phi_{j \to i}^{(m)} = \Phi_m(i) - \Phi_m(j)$:
\begin{equation}
\langle \tilde{q}_i, \, \tilde{k}_j \rangle = \langle q_i, \, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}} + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}} (q_i^{(m)})^\top \mathbf{R}\Big( (j - i)\theta_m + \big(\Phi_m(j) - \Phi_m(i)\big) \Big) k_j^{(m)}
\end{equation}
When the cumulative phase flow across evolution channels reaches an anti-phase half-turn ($\Delta \Phi_{j \to i}^{(m)} = \Phi_m(i) - \Phi_m(j) = \pi$), $\mathbf{R}((j - i)\theta_m - \pi) = -\mathbf{R}((j - i)\theta_m)$, yielding exact evolution-subspace sign inversion:
\begin{equation}
\langle \tilde{q}_i, \, \tilde{k}_j \rangle = \langle q_i, \, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}} - \langle q_i, \, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By orthogonality of the decomposition $\mathbb{R}^d = \mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$, the inner product decomposes as:
\begin{equation}
\langle \tilde{q}_i, \, \tilde{k}_j \rangle = \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}}} \langle \tilde{q}_i^{(m)}, \, \tilde{k}_j^{(m)} \rangle + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}} \langle \tilde{q}_i^{(m)}, \, \tilde{k}_j^{(m)} \rangle
\end{equation}
For all channels $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}}$, the phase flow is frozen ($\Phi_m \equiv 0$), recovering the baseline RoPE inner product $\langle q_i, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}}$. For $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$, expanding the inner product using planar rotation orthogonality $\mathbf{R}(\alpha)^\top \mathbf{R}(\beta) = \mathbf{R}(\beta - \alpha)$:
\begin{align}
\langle \tilde{q}_i^{(m)}, \, \tilde{k}_j^{(m)} \rangle &= (\tilde{q}_i^{(m)})^\top \tilde{k}_j^{(m)} \nonumber \\
&= (q_i^{(m)})^\top \mathbf{R}(i\theta_m + \Phi_m(i))^\top \mathbf{R}(j\theta_m + \Phi_m(j)) k_j^{(m)} \nonumber \\
&= (q_i^{(m)})^\top \mathbf{R}\Big( (j - i)\theta_m + \big(\Phi_m(j) - \Phi_m(i)\big) \Big) k_j^{(m)}
\end{align}
Because $\Phi_m(i) - \Phi_m(j) = \Delta \Phi_{j \to i}^{(m)}$, the relative rotation angle is $(j - i)\theta_m + (\Phi_m(j) - \Phi_m(i)) = (j - i)\theta_m - \Delta \Phi_{j \to i}^{(m)}$. When $\Delta \Phi_{j \to i}^{(m)} = \pi$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{R}((j - i)\theta_m - \pi) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos((j-i)\theta_m - \pi) & -\sin((j-i)\theta_m - \pi) \\ \sin((j-i)\theta_m - \pi) & \cos((j-i)\theta_m - \pi) \end{pmatrix} = -\mathbf{R}((j - i)\theta_m)
\end{equation}
Summing over $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$ yields $-\langle q_i, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}}$. Substituting this back gives $\langle \tilde{q}_i, \tilde{k}_j \rangle = \langle q_i, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}} - \langle q_i, k_j \rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}}^{\mathrm{RoPE}}$, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous Lipschitz Continuity Bound of Rotation Operator]
\label{thm:lipschitz}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_m(t) \triangleq \mathbf{R}(t\theta_m + \Phi_m(t))$ be the continuous modulated rotation operator on subspace $m$, and let $\Delta\Phi_m(t+1) \triangleq \Phi_m(t+1) - \Phi_m(t)$ denote the effective single-step phase increment bounded by $|\Delta\Phi_m(t+1)| \le \tilde{\omega}_{\max} \triangleq (1 + 2\eta)\omega_{\max}$ (which reduces to $\tilde{\omega}_{\max} = \omega_{\max}$ for unquantized phase $\eta = 0$). For any adjacent sequence positions $t$ and $t+1$ satisfying the stability condition $\theta_m + \tilde{\omega}_{\max} \le \pi$, the operator difference satisfies the exact spectral norm bound:
\begin{equation}
\|\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_m(t+1) - \tilde{\mathbf{R}}_m(t)\|_2 \le 2\sin\left(\frac{\theta_m + \tilde{\omega}_{\max}}{2}\right)
\end{equation}
More generally, for unconstrained frequencies, the universal bound is $2\sin\left(\frac{\min(\pi, \, \theta_m + \tilde{\omega}_{\max})}{2}\right)$. For standard RoPE heads where $\theta_m \le 1.0$ and $\tilde{\omega}_{\max} \le \pi - 1 \approx 2.14$, the condition $\theta_m + \tilde{\omega}_{\max} \le \pi$ holds unconditionally.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any 2D planar rotation matrices $\mathbf{R}(\alpha), \mathbf{R}(\beta) \in \mathrm{SO}(2)$, factoring out $\mathbf{R}(\beta)$ yields:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{R}(\alpha) - \mathbf{R}(\beta) = \mathbf{R}(\beta) \left( \mathbf{R}(\alpha - \beta) - \mathbf{I} \right)
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{R}(\beta)$ is an orthogonal matrix, its spectral norm is $\|\mathbf{R}(\beta)\|_2 = 1$. The eigenvalues of $\mathbf{R}(\Delta) - \mathbf{I}$ with $\Delta \triangleq \alpha - \beta$ are $e^{\pm i \Delta} - 1$. The modulus of each eigenvalue evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
|e^{i \Delta} - 1| = \sqrt{(\cos\Delta - 1)^2 + \sin^2\Delta} = \sqrt{2 - 2\cos\Delta} = 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)\right|
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{R}(\Delta) - \mathbf{I}$ is normal, its spectral norm equals its spectral radius:
\begin{equation}
\|\mathbf{R}(\alpha) - \mathbf{R}(\beta)\|_2 = 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha - \beta}{2}\right)\right|
\end{equation}
Setting $\alpha = (t+1)\theta_m + \Phi_m(t+1)$ and $\beta = t\theta_m + \Phi_m(t)$, and applying the Mean Value Theorem to $f(\bar{\Phi}) = \bar{\Phi} - \eta\sin(2\bar{\Phi})$ where $|f'(\bar{\Phi})| = |1 - 2\eta\cos(2\bar{\Phi})| \le 1 + 2\eta$, the effective phase step satisfies:
\begin{equation}
|\alpha - \beta| = |\theta_m + \Delta\Phi_m(t+1)| \le \theta_m + (1 + 2\eta)|\omega_m(t+1)| \le \theta_m + \tilde{\omega}_{\max} \le \pi
\end{equation}
By monotonicity of $\sin(x/2)$ on $[0, \pi]$, we establish:
\begin{equation}
\|\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_m(t+1) - \tilde{\mathbf{R}}_m(t)\|_2 \le 2\sin\left(\frac{\theta_m + \tilde{\omega}_{\max}}{2}\right)
\end{equation}
which guarantees uniform Lipschitz continuity along the causal sequence trajectory.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Pairwise Target-to-Decoy Odds Ratio Scaling]
\label{thm:odds_ratio}
Let query $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ attend over historical keys $l \in \{0, \dots, i\}$ within a single attention head via Softmax attention. Let the baseline RoPE-rotated subspace logit components be defined as:
\begin{equation}
A_{il}^{\text{anchor}} \triangleq \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\text{anchor}}} (q_i^{(m)})^\top \mathbf{R}\big((l-i)\theta_m\big) k_l^{(m)}, \quad
A_{il}^{\text{mob}} \triangleq \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}} (q_i^{(m)})^\top \mathbf{R}\big((l-i)\theta_m\big) k_l^{(m)}
\end{equation}
Assume subspace-coherent phase locking across $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$ such that an operative target key $k$ remains in phase with query $i$ ($\Delta\Phi_{k\to i}^{(m)} \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$), while an invalidated decoy key $j$ undergoes a uniform anti-phase shift ($\Delta\Phi_{j\to i}^{(m)} \equiv \pi \pmod{2\pi}$).
Then, within each head, the background partition function $\tilde{Z}_i$ strictly cancels in the pairwise probability ratio $\mathrm{Odds}(k:j\mid i) \triangleq P(k\mid i)/P(j\mid i)$, yielding:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{mob}}(k : j \mid i)}{\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{std}}(k : j \mid i)} = \exp\!\left(2 A_{ij}^{\text{mob}}\right), \qquad
\frac{\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{mob}}(j : k \mid i)}{\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{std}}(j : k \mid i)} = \exp\!\left(-2 A_{ij}^{\text{mob}}\right)
\end{equation}
In particular, strict decoy odds attenuation ($\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{mob}}(j:k\mid i) < \mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{std}}(j:k\mid i)$) holds if and only if the baseline evolution-subspace alignment is positive ($A_{ij}^{\text{mob}} > 0$). When long-range positional rotation induces $A_{ij}^{\text{mob}} < 0$, an unconditional $\pi$-shift inverts the sign to $+|A_{ij}^{\text{mob}}|$, motivating a sign-aware gating rule $\Delta\Phi_{j\to i}^{\text{eff}} = \pi \cdot \mathbb{I}(A_{ij}^{\text{mob}} > 0)$ or choosing $\mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$ purely within the ultra-low-frequency regime where $(i-j)\theta_m \ll \pi/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any pair of tokens $k$ and $j$, the pairwise odds ratio under the Softmax distribution within a given attention head is:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Odds}(k : j \mid i) \triangleq \frac{P(k \mid i)}{P(j \mid i)} = \frac{\exp(\tilde{A}_{ik}) / \tilde{Z}_i}{\exp(\tilde{A}_{ij}) / \tilde{Z}_i} = \frac{\exp(\tilde{A}_{ik})}{\exp(\tilde{A}_{ij})} = \exp(\tilde{A}_{ik} - \tilde{A}_{ij})
\end{equation}
Notice that the head partition function $\tilde{Z}_i = \sum_{l'=0}^i \exp(\tilde{A}_{il'})$ cancels strictly and identically from numerator and denominator without any approximation.

Under standard RoPE, the unmodulated logits are $A_{ik}^{\text{std}} = A_{ik}^{\text{anchor}} + A_{ik}^{\text{mob}}$ and $A_{ij}^{\text{std}} = A_{ij}^{\text{anchor}} + A_{ij}^{\text{mob}}$, yielding:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Odds}_{\text{std}}(k : j \mid i) = \exp\left( (A_{ik}^{\text{anchor}} + A_{ik}^{\text{mob}}) - (A_{ij}^{\text{anchor}} + A_{ij}^{\text{mob}}) \right)
\end{equation}
Under SG-M\"obius, because target $k$ is in phase ($\Delta\Phi_{k \to i} = 0$), its logit is invariant: $\tilde{A}_{ik} = A_{ik}^{\text{anchor}} + A_{ik}^{\text{mob}}$. For decoy $j$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:trans_inv}, the anti-phase half-turn inverts the evolution subspace logit: $\tilde{A}_{ij} = A_{ij}^{\text{anchor}} - A_{ij}^{\text{mob}}$. The modulated pairwise odds evaluate to:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{mob}}(k : j \mid i) = \exp\left( (A_{ik}^{\text{anchor}} + A_{ik}^{\text{mob}}) - (A_{ij}^{\text{anchor}} - A_{ij}^{\text{mob}}) \right)
\end{equation}
Dividing the modulated odds by the baseline odds:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{mob}}(k : j \mid i)}{\mathrm{Odds}_{\mathrm{std}}(k : j \mid i)} = \frac{\exp(A_{ik}^{\text{std}} - A_{ij}^{\text{anchor}} + A_{ij}^{\text{mob}})}{\exp(A_{ik}^{\text{std}} - A_{ij}^{\text{anchor}} - A_{ij}^{\text{mob}})} = \exp\left( 2 A_{ij}^{\text{mob}} \right)
\end{equation}
Inverting the ratio yields the decoy odds scaling factor $\exp(-2 A_{ij}^{\text{mob}})$, which is strictly less than 1 if and only if $A_{ij}^{\text{mob}} > 0$, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Causal KV-Cache Invariance and Equivalence}
A critical requirement in practical LLM deployment is maintaining strict compatibility with autoregressive key-value (KV) caching without recomputing historical states.

\begin{proposition}[Prefill vs Autoregressive KV-Cache Decoding Equivalence]
\label{prop:kv_cache}
Let $\mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}} \in \{0, 1\}^{d/2}$ be the subspace mask ($\mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}}[m] = \mathbb{I}(m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}})$), $\bar{\Phi}(t) = \sum_{\tau=0}^t \omega(\tau)$ be the causal prefix sum of smoothed phase velocity, and $\Phi(t) = \bar{\Phi}(t) - \eta\sin(2\bar{\Phi}(t))$ be the pointwise quantized phase. Define the full modulated orthogonal rotation operator:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{R}}(t) \triangleq \bigoplus_{m=1}^{d/2} \mathbf{R}\big(t\theta_m + \mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}}[m]\Phi(t)\big) \in \mathrm{SO}(d)
\end{equation}
For any sequence length $L$, the attention logit matrix computed in batch parallel prefill mode:
\begin{align}
A_{\mathrm{prefill}}[i, j] &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum_{m=1}^{d/2} \left( \mathbf{R}(i\theta_m + \mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}}[m]\Phi(i)) q_{i,m} \right)^\top \left( \mathbf{R}(j\theta_m + \mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}}[m]\Phi(j)) k_{j,m} \right) \nonumber \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \left( \tilde{\mathbf{R}}(i) q_i \right)^\top \left( \tilde{\mathbf{R}}(j) k_j \right)
\end{align}
is identically equivalent to the logit computed during step-by-step autoregressive decoding using the cached key $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{cache}}[j] = \tilde{\mathbf{R}}(j) k_j$:
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{decode}}[i, j] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \tilde{q}_i^\top \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{cache}}[j] \equiv A_{\mathrm{prefill}}[i, j], \qquad \forall 0 \le j \le i < L
\end{equation}
with maximum numerical drift bounded strictly by floating-point roundoff ($< 10^{-12}$). In particular, cached keys $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{cache}}[j]$ remain strictly immutable as future tokens $i > j$ arrive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The cached key $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{cache}}[j] = \tilde{\mathbf{R}}(j) k_j$ depends strictly on historical inputs $h_\tau$ for $\tau \le j$. Because both the prefix sum $\bar{\Phi}(j) = \sum_{\tau=0}^j \omega(\tau)$ and the pointwise projection $\Phi(j)$ are strictly causal, future phase velocities $\omega(\tau)$ for $\tau > j$ do not affect $\Phi(j)$. During decoding at step $i \ge j$, the query $\tilde{q}_i = \tilde{\mathbf{R}}(i) q_i$ computes its inner product directly against the immutable cached vector $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{cache}}[j]$. By associativity and bilinearity of matrix multiplication, the resulting scalar matches $A_{\mathrm{prefill}}[i, j]$ to machine precision, requiring zero retroactive cache invalidation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-Stage Parallel Associative Scan and FP16 Stability Bound]
\label{prop:associative_scan}
Let $H = (h_0, h_1, \dots, h_{L-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$ be the prefill hidden sequence. The causal phase flow $\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^L$ is computed via a two-stage parallel associative scan:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (Affine EMA Scan)}: Let $g_t = \omega_{\max}\tanh(W_g h_t + b_g)$. The IIR filter $\omega_t = (1-\nu)\omega_{t-1} + \nu g_t$ with initial boundary state $\omega_{-1}=0$ is equivalent to prefix evaluation of affine operators $(a_t, b_t) = (1-\nu, \, \nu g_t)$ under the associative binary operator $(a_2, b_2) \otimes (a_1, b_1) = (a_2 a_1, \, a_2 b_1 + b_2)$, evaluated in $\mathcal{O}(\log L)$ parallel depth.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (Additive Prefix Scan)}: The cumulative unconstrained phase $\bar{\Phi}_t = \sum_{\tau=0}^t \omega_\tau$ with $\bar{\Phi}_{-1}=0$ is evaluated via a parallel prefix sum under standard addition $(\mathbb{R}, +)$.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3 (Pointwise Phase Locking)}: $\Phi_t = \bar{\Phi}_t - \eta\sin(2\bar{\Phi}_t)$ is applied point-wise in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ depth.
\end{enumerate}
In IEEE 754 half-precision (FP16) arithmetic with unit roundoff $\epsilon_{\text{mach}} \approx 9.77 \times 10^{-4}$, the accumulated floating-point roundoff error over tree depth $D = \lceil \log_2 L \rceil$ satisfies the analytical bound:
\begin{equation}
|\Delta \Phi_L| \le 2 D \epsilon_{\text{mach}} + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\text{mach}}^2) = \mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\text{mach}} \log L)
\end{equation}
For $L \le 18,030$ ($D = 15$), $|\Delta \Phi_L| \le 0.029\,\text{rad} \approx 1.68^\circ$, which is strictly bounded inside the phase-locking attractor basin $|\delta| < \frac{\pi}{6}$ where $-\eta\sin(2\bar{\Phi})$ dynamically suppresses roundoff drift.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{System Implementation via Epilogue Angle Fusion.}
Modern inference engines (e.g., FlashAttention, vLLM) apply rotary embeddings prior to the attention GEMM. Because $\Phi_h(t)$ depends causally on the layer hidden state $h_t$, it cannot be precomputed statically offline. During batch prefill ($L \gg 1$), we evaluate the scalar projection $g_h(h_t)$, execute the Two-Stage Associative Scan (Proposition~\ref{prop:associative_scan}) in parallel across the sequence to obtain $\Phi_h(t)$. During single-step autoregressive decoding, we fuse the scalar phase velocity projection $g_h(h_t)$, $O(1)$ recurrence update, and subspace-masked angle offset
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\Theta}_{h,t} = t\Theta + \Phi_h(t)\mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}}, \quad \mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}} \in \{0, 1\}^{d/2}
\end{equation}
directly into the QKV projection epilogue prior to launching the rotary embedding kernel (or equivalently via the $2\times 2$ trigonometric addition identity $\mathbf{R}(t\theta_m + \Phi_h(t)) = \mathbf{R}(\Phi_h(t))\mathbf{R}(t\theta_m)$ on $m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}}$ using the static precomputed RoPE table $\mathbf{R}(t\theta_m)$). Consequently, the core attention kernel (Query-Key GEMM, causal masking, Softmax, and Value reduction) executes with identical $O(1)$ per-step KV-cache memory access patterns and zero retroactive cache rewrites.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Autoregressive Generation with Subspace Anti-Phase KV-Cache}
\label{alg:kv_decoding}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} Prior boundary states $\omega_{-1} \gets 0$, cumulative phase $\bar{\Phi}_{-1} \gets 0$, subspace mask $\mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}} \in \{0, 1\}^{d/2}$ ($\mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}}[m] = \mathbb{I}(m \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{mob}})$), Cache $\mathbf{K} \gets [], \mathbf{V} \gets []$.
\FOR{each decoding step $i = 0, 1, 2, \dots$}
    \STATE Receive layer hidden state $h_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$, compute raw query $q_i$, key $k_i$, value $v_i$.
    \STATE Evaluate continuous phase velocity projection: $g_i \gets \omega_{\max}\tanh(W_g h_i + b_g) \in [-\omega_{\max}, \omega_{\max}]$.
    \STATE Update smoothed phase velocity: $\omega_i \gets (1 - \nu)\omega_{i-1} + \nu g_i$.
    \STATE Update cumulative unconstrained phase: $\bar{\Phi}_i \gets \bar{\Phi}_{i-1} + \omega_i$.
    \STATE Apply phase-locking quantizer: $\Phi_i \gets \bar{\Phi}_i - \eta \sin(2\bar{\Phi}_i)$.
    \STATE Construct modulated rotation matrix: $\tilde{\mathbf{R}}(i) \triangleq \bigoplus_{m=1}^{d/2} \mathbf{R}(i\theta_m + \mathbf{m}_{\text{sub}}[m]\Phi_i)$.
    \STATE Modulate key: $\tilde{k}_i \gets \tilde{\mathbf{R}}(i) k_i$.
    \STATE Append to KV-Cache: $\mathbf{K} \gets [\mathbf{K}; \, \tilde{k}_i]$, $\mathbf{V} \gets [\mathbf{V}; \, v_i]$ \COMMENT{Past keys remain strictly immutable}
    \STATE Modulate query: $\tilde{q}_i \gets \tilde{\mathbf{R}}(i) q_i$.
    \STATE Compute causal attention: $\mathbf{a}_i \gets \mathrm{Softmax}\left( \frac{\tilde{q}_i^\top \mathbf{K}}{\sqrt{d}} \right)$, $\mathbf{o}_i \gets \mathbf{a}_i \mathbf{V}$.
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

As formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:kv_decoding}, SG-M\"obius requires zero retroactive invalidation of cached keys in GPU memory. The incremental cost at step $i$ is exactly one scalar addition and planar angle rotation ($O(1)$ time and memory complexity).

For the terminal probe query at position $i = L-1$, we measure:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decoy Attention ($A_{\mathrm{decoy}}$)}: Multi-head mean Softmax attention probability allocated to the superseded decoy premise token span by the probe query at $L-1$, $A_{\mathrm{decoy}} = \frac{1}{|H|} \sum_{h \in H} A^{(h)}_{L-1, t_{\mathrm{decoy}}}$.
    \item \textbf{Target Attention ($A_{\mathrm{target}}$)}: Multi-head mean Softmax attention probability allocated to the overriding target premise token span, $A_{\mathrm{target}} = \frac{1}{|H|} \sum_{h \in H} A^{(h)}_{L-1, t_{\mathrm{target}}}$.
    \item \textbf{Attention Contrast Ratio (ACR)}: Multi-head aggregate attention contrast ratio $\mathrm{ACR} \triangleq A_{\mathrm{target}} / (A_{\mathrm{decoy}} + \epsilon)$, alongside the single-head pairwise odds ratio $\mathrm{Odds}^{(h)}(t_2 : t_1 \mid L-1) = A^{(h)}_{L-1, t_2} / A^{(h)}_{L-1, t_1}$ governed directly by Theorem~\ref{thm:odds_ratio}.
    \item \textbf{Decoy Suppression Factor}: Relative attenuation factor $A_{\mathrm{decoy}}^{\mathrm{std}} / A_{\mathrm{decoy}}^{\mathrm{mob}}$.
    \item \textbf{Neutral Perplexity (PPL)}: Perplexity evaluated on smooth WikiText-103 text without inversions to verify absence of false-suppression degradation.
\end{itemize}

Under our proposed SG-M\"obius formulation with dynamic frequency cutoff $\alpha(L)$, obsolete decoy attention is strictly and monotonically attenuated across all evaluated context lengths ($1.13\times\text{--}1.90\times$ physical suppression), driving substantial Attention Contrast Ratio gains across the entire sequence domain. \section{Conclusion and Limitations}
We presented Subspace Anti-Phase Attention (SG-M\"obius-RoPE), an attention formulation extending Rotary Position Embedding via causal orthogonal subspace phase modulation $\mathbb{R}^d = \mathcal{S}_{\text{anchor}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\text{mob}}$. By accumulating smooth phase flow from a continuous phase velocity $\omega_{h,m}(t) \in [-\omega_{\max}, \omega_{\max}]$ with 1D viscous smoothing and a single-step phase-locking quantizer, SG-M\"obius provides destructive interference under narrative inversion ($\Delta \Phi = \pi$, $\mathbf{R}(\theta - \pi) = -\mathbf{R}(\theta)$) and parity restoration under double negation ($\Delta \Phi = 2\pi$, $\mathbf{R}(\theta - 2\pi) = \mathbf{R}(\theta)$). Invariants and algorithmic equivalence are formally verified in Lean~4 and Z3 SMT solvers. Empirical evaluations on Qwen3.5-9B (dual Nvidia T4 GPUs, FP16) demonstrate strictly monotonic decoy attention suppression ($1.13\times\text{--}1.90\times$), up to $+465.1\%$ multi-head ACR gain ($3.62 \to 20.44$), near-identical $O(1)$ KV-cache latency via epilogue angle fusion ($1.8\text{--}2.1\,\mu\text{s}$ per-layer operator overhead), and baseline perplexity preservation ($7.48$, $0.0\%$ degradation).

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work.}
The current realization employs 1D viscous smoothing with continuous phase velocity bounded by $|\omega(t)| \le \omega_{\max}$. In highly ambiguous or domain-specialized texts, semantic projection heads may require task-specific calibration. Promising future research includes end-to-end differentiable phase projection learning during foundation pre-training, where continuous phase fields are jointly optimized across attention heads, as well as extending subspace phase modulation dynamics to multidimensional vision (2D ViTs) and video architectures.
